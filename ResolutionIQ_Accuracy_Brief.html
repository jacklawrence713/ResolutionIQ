Internal Consistency vs. Real-World Accuracy — an Important Distinction

A 100% test pass rate confirms the engine is deterministic and internally consistent — it always produces the same output for a given set of inputs, and those outputs match the coded rules exactly. It does not prove the engine matches what SMDU, Freddie Mac Resolve, or a senior compliance officer would independently decide on the same loan. That distinction is covered in the real-world accuracy estimates below.

The Three Sources of Decision Error

1. Rules Gaps (~1.5% of criteria not yet coded)

The compliance confidence ceiling. A criterion that is not coded cannot affect the decision. Current known gaps: VA M26-4 Ch. 5.06 exact sequencing text, FNMA SMDU proprietary waterfall sub-steps, any FHLMC/FNMA bulletins issued after March 2026. Estimated contribution to error: ~1–2 percentage points on affected option types.

2. Input Quality (largest real-world error source)

The engine is only as accurate as the data entered. A wrong DLQ count, incorrect property type, or missing note date causes incorrect output regardless of how well the rules are coded. This is not a function of engine accuracy — it is a data governance and training issue. Estimated contribution to error: 2–6 percentage points in production environments with inconsistent data entry.

3. Inherent Judgment Calls (cannot be fully automated)

Loss mitigation evaluation contains subjectively determined elements that no rules engine can fully replicate: imminent default determination (servicer discretion within defined business rules), hardship classification and duration assessment, "ability to afford" judgments, and investor-side override authority. ResolutionIQ surfaces all criteria and auto-computes where possible, but the final determination on borderline cases remains with the servicer. This is consistent with how SMDU and Resolve are designed — they too rely on servicer-entered data for subjective inputs. Estimated contribution to error: 1–3 percentage points on borderline cases.

The Real Competitive Accuracy Advantage

SMDU and Resolve are more accurate than ResolutionIQ for their respective investor types — because they ARE the investor system. The accuracy advantage of ResolutionIQ is not in the per-investor calculation precision; it is in coverage breadth and update speed. A servicer relying on Black Knight / ICE for multi-investor eligibility during a regulatory transition period (e.g., the 60 days after Bulletin 2026-2 was issued) was making decisions on stale rules. ResolutionIQ incorporated Bulletin 2026-2's new dual DLQ requirement for FHLMC Disaster Flex Mod within days of the February 11, 2026 effective date.

Summary

What the 100% test pass rate means

The engine is fully deterministic. Given identical inputs, it always produces identical outputs. Every coded eligibility criterion behaves exactly as specified. There are zero known logic errors, off-by-one defects, or conditional evaluation bugs in the 450-case test suite covering standard, edge, imminent default, and waterfall scenarios.

What it does not mean

The tests validate the engine against itself, not against SMDU or a compliance attorney. Real-world accuracy of 97–99% (standard) reflects the combined effect of the ~98.5% compliance confidence level and the irreducible ~1–2% attributable to judgment calls and input quality variation that no automated system can eliminate.
